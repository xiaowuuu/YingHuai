    By the Bin: Map-based sharing for sustainable living

    A community-driven platform where users can post and discover free items left around the city. By turning street finds into shared treasures, the app encourages reuse and sustainable living.

    Context

    This project began from observing community sharing practices through the Facebook group Meadows Share, where locals exchange unwanted items. While the idea promotes sustainability, I noticed usability issues—posts were often outdated, and finding nearby items was inconvenient. Inspired by my own experiences discovering usable items left on the street, I designed a map-based platform that simplifies how people share, browse, and collect items locally. The goal is to make reuse more intuitive and accessible, reducing waste while strengthening community connections.

    My role: Designer, user researcher, developer

    Users can explore available items nearby, post their own finds, and contribute to a community-driven cycle of sharing and sustainability.

    Sketching & Wireframing

    Usability Testing

    Tested initial designs with target users using task-based scenarios. Identified usability issues and pain points, then iterated on the design to improve user experience based on testing insights.

    User Insight

    1. Many users enjoy browsing but rarely collect items due to lack of real-time updates or casual curiosity.
    2. Enthusiastic users want to quickly see nearby items while they’re already out and about.
    3. Sharing items feels rewarding — users enjoy giving and seeing others benefit.
    4. Users are forgiving about item quality or availability since everything is free.
